> For those of us who have lived with depression, or are still finding our way through it. These words are not answers or medical advice. They are simply what someone who has walked part of this road would like to say while sitting beside you. Please keep following your doctor's guidance; and if things feel especially hard right now, please let someone you trust stay with you.

I lived with depression for ten years. People sometimes say that long illness makes you an expert, but I would never call myself one. I am not a doctor, and I do not have a way to make everyone well. I only remember how long and heavy those days could feel, and how, little by little, I found my way to today. So I wanted to write some of it down. Maybe one line will make you feel, “Someone else has felt this, too.” If so, these words have found their way to the right place.

Along the road to recovery, I often found myself returning to the same place: the thing in my heart I could not get past. It might be regret, remorse, something I could not do no matter how hard I tried, or a pain I could not quite put into words but carried with me all the same. Sometimes it felt like a nightmare I could not wake from. Other people might not see it, but we know how real it is. And it can feel so helpless when our thoughts, routines, habits, and circumstances become tangled together. The harder we try to pull free, the more trapped we feel. Finding a way through is not easy. If you cannot do it just yet, please do not rush to blame yourself. This is not the kind of knot that “just think positive” can undo.

I used to think I had to understand everything and untangle every thought before I could get better. It took time to realize that life does not always wait for us to solve what hurts before it carries on. Sometimes treatment and the people around me held me up first. Sometimes something very small in an ordinary day lifted my eyes, for a moment, from the thoughts I was caught in. Medication, treatment, and support mattered to me. I also began to wonder: what might help me feel even a little more connected to life?

For me, sometimes that small connection was love. Not a sudden, shining love for myself or the whole world, but the quiet practice of saying something kind in my heart. When I went outside, I looked up at the sky and felt the sun on my skin. I noticed the flowers and grass by the road, and wished the people hurrying past me well. In that moment, the world did not suddenly become better, and my troubles did not disappear. I just felt a little less shut away inside them. This is only what happened for me, not a method or a thing anyone has to do. If you cannot do it right now, you do not have to force yourself. You do not have to become optimistic before you deserve love.

I did not wake up one day and discover that I was completely well. More often, I found a little more of myself in the middle of ordinary days. Eventually, I understood that depression was an illness I had lived through, but it was not all of who I am. When my mood sinks again, I try to pause and ask myself: Am I exhausted? Do I need to talk to someone? Is there something I should discuss with my doctor? If you are taking medication, please share your concerns with the doctor who prescribed it rather than stopping or changing the dose on your own. Caring for ourselves is not giving up, or admitting that we are not strong enough. Sometimes it is the moment we finally stop trying to carry everything alone.

Little by little, I noticed that when there was a little room inside me, I could see more of the life in front of me. And small changes in everyday life could, in turn, make a little more room inside. Maybe it was resting a little longer than the day before, or finally telling someone, “I'm not doing very well.” These things may seem small, but they are ways life reaches back toward us.

If you feel like it, look at the world around you with me for a moment. You do not have to love it right away, or find some meaning in it. Just look. Maybe it has been a long time since you slept well or really rested. You have been trying so hard. When you can, let yourself rest for a while. If sleep does not come tonight, that is not your fault. When you wake, you can meet the real person you are, slowly. You do not have to like yourself immediately or figure everything out. Just make a little room for yourself to be here.

I have come to feel that living honestly matters more than living “correctly.” We are imperfect, and so is the world. Sometimes getting through today is already a lot. Perhaps one day you will want to do something for the world again, to make it a little more beautiful, a little more loving. Perhaps that day still feels far away. That is all right. We do not have to hurry ourselves along.

As I write this, I think of a question I often hear: Is there really a God? Where is God? I have wondered about that, too. One answer stayed with me: “First, congratulations on beginning to ask the question. And what comes next? Well, what comes next comes next.” I like it because it does not rush to answer for me. Whether or not to believe in God is personal. For me, a church, a prayer, or simply imagining that love is still somewhere in the world can sometimes be a place to rest for a while. If any of that comforts you, I hope you can rest there. If it does not, that is all right, too. You can look for a little light in your own way.

I also know that there are times when it feels as if no light can reach us. When pain runs deep, the future can disappear from view. We may even think about dying and feel there is no other way. If that is where you are right now, I do not want to brush your pain aside with “just keep going.” I would rather ask you not to stay alone with it: message someone you trust, even if all you can say is, “I'm having a hard time. Could you stay with me for a while?” You can also contact your doctor, a mental-health professional, or a local crisis service. If you are afraid you might hurt yourself, or cannot stay safe right now, contact your local emergency services or go to an emergency department, and ask someone you trust to stay with you. You do not have to explain everything before you deserve help. Could we just get through this moment together?

I cannot carry your pain for you, and I would never claim to know exactly what you are going through. But I believe that what you say you feel is real, and I am willing to listen. We do not have to save one another. If we can keep each other company for a little while on the hardest stretch of the road, that is already something precious.

END - FRIEND: Even “Friend” eventually reaches an “end.” I hope that even if we do not meet often, we remember that we were friends—and comrades—for a while on a difficult road. In those days, perhaps we had no answers, but we did walk beside each other for part of the way.

When you begin to feel better, perhaps it will be time for us to wave goodbye and return to our busy, real lives. Until then, save some of the love in your heart for yourself, and share what you can with your family, friends, and colleagues. I hope we can all find peace and health, and live our lives as fully as we can.

I once heard someone say: “Being ill does not mean you did something wrong or hurt someone and are being punished. Maybe you have gone too long without caring for yourself—keeping your body tense, working too hard, and holding your sadness and hurt inside.” Those words once made me feel that perhaps I did not have to take all the blame onto myself. I do not know whether they explain anyone else's experience, but they reminded me that illness is not anyone's fault, and that we deserve to be treated gently.

May we learn to forgive others and allow others to forgive us. May we remember to be grateful and give one another the chance to return kindness. Everything you have lived through is part of your story, but it is not all of who you are. You are one of a kind, and you have the right to find the life you want, slowly and in your own time.

As a friend, there is still so much I want to say. But let me stop here for now. I hope that one day we will meet again in the lives we have made for ourselves, with a little more sunshine around us and a little more peace in our hearts.

I hope things grow gentler for you, little by little. And if they cannot just yet, please remember: you do not have to walk alone.
